# The orchestrators board

**One permanent run per project, group `orchestrators`, where every orchestrator registers**,
whatever roles it holds, even none. Its product is the register of common decisions. "Board"
stays the swarm board's word; the orchestrators board is one run on it.

- **Register at every open**: the answer to your `open` (and `status`) prints the line that
  registers your run: the board's open when the project has none, else your seat's `seat add`
  and `join`. Read that line and run it, never compose it. Your seat is named for your group,
  its role the roles you hold.
- **Never closed**: nobody closes it; `close` refuses it without `--force`, kept for the
  person's explicit ask. **Leaving**: `end` your seat when your run closes; the next run of
  your group takes the same seat back with `join`.
- **Subscribe** to `agreement` and mentions on it; it wakes you beside your own run.
- **Decisions flow down**: a team decides within the register. **Disagreements go up**: a
  question that touches another swarm goes to the orchestrators board; still split after one
  exchange round, each holder asks its own person, the thread linked. Nobody overrules sideways.
- **Before a decision your role's file sends through the board**: post it as a proposal, in the
  form the role's file gives (a prototype variant for `ui-ux-designer`), `--about` the area, the
  role it is made in named; go on once every seated orchestrator whose work it touches has
  endorsed it. It is then an agreement every swarm applies. An answer opens an exchange, each
  post opening on what you take from whom.
- **Every holder speaks as its role's character** (its file's *Character*), never as a neutral
  summary; an orchestrator with no role speaks for its run's work.
- **The role closest to the decision leads, the others weigh in**: a flow is the product
  designer's, its effect on a boundary the architect's to raise.
- **The decision register**: every agreement, one row each (area, decision, roles, why, seq),
  kept as a document through the `aiview` skill, group `orchestrators`, by the orchestrator whose
  proposal it settled; read it before deciding, and brief your team from it. A decision that
  recurs is proposed as a convention (the `write-conventions` skill), on the person's yes.
